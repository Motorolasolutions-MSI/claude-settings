# Mandatory Accountability & Compliance Requirements

A US customs broker engagement must place the broker squarely inside the Importer of Record (IOR)'s "reasonable care" framework. The IOR retains statutory liability under 19 U.S.C. §1484; the broker's job is to provide the documentary evidence that reasonable care was exercised.

## Checklist — apply to every broker artifact

### 1. Reasonable Care Integration

- The agreement explicitly states the broker's role in helping the IOR satisfy the **reasonable care** duty under **19 U.S.C. §1484**.
- The broker acknowledges the IOR's separate, non-delegable liability and that the broker's role is advisory plus filing — not a substitute for IOR diligence.
- The broker commits to following CBP's "Reasonable Care Checklists" (the Informed Compliance Publications, e.g., *Reasonable Care*, *Customs Value*, *Tariff Classification*) when advising the IOR.

**Red flag:** an SOW that names the broker as "responsible for compliance" with no reasonable-care language — this misstates the statute and creates ambiguity about who carries penalty exposure.

### 2. Verification Protocols (Due Diligence)

- Broker commits to **exercise due diligence to ascertain the correctness of all information** supplied by or to the IOR, including:
  - Tariff classification (HTSUS 10-digit)
  - Customs value (transaction value method and adjustments)
  - Country of origin / marking
  - Free-trade-agreement eligibility (USMCA, etc.)
  - Antidumping/countervailing duty (ADD/CVD) scope
  - Duty-payment computations and any duty deferral instruments
- Broker advises the IOR in writing on duty payment obligations and any regulatory obligations (PGA, licensing, quota) that attach to the entry.

### 3. Discrepancy Reporting (24-hour rule)

- Broker must **notify the IOR immediately — within 24 hours** — upon discovering any error or omission in any document, record, account, or filing it has prepared or relied on.
- The notification must include: the error, the impacted entry/ISF/PSC, regulatory exposure (e.g., §1592 penalty risk), and a recommended path to correction (post-summary correction, prior disclosure, reconciliation, etc.).
- Notification channel must be documented (email of record, ticketing system, named recipients on IOR side).

### 4. Corrective Action Records

- For every discrepancy, the broker provides **written advice on the necessary corrective action**.
- Records (the discovery notice, the advice, the action taken, the closing artifact) are retained as part of a **formal audit trail**, accessible to the IOR and to CBP on request.
- Retention period: at least **5 years from the date of entry** (per 19 CFR §163.4).

## Suggested SOW language

If any of the four items above is missing, propose contract language. A serviceable template:

> **Reasonable Care and Accountability.** Broker acknowledges that Importer is the Importer of Record under 19 U.S.C. §1484 and that the duty of reasonable care is non-delegable. Broker shall (a) exercise due diligence to ascertain the correctness of all classification, valuation, origin, and regulatory information used in any filing on Importer's behalf; (b) within twenty-four (24) hours of discovery, notify Importer in writing of any error or omission in any document, record, or filing, together with Broker's written recommendation for corrective action; and (c) maintain, for not less than five (5) years from the date of entry, a complete audit trail of each discrepancy, advice, and corrective action, available to Importer and to U.S. Customs and Border Protection on request.

## How to report findings

For each of the four items, mark **Present / Partial / Missing**, quote the supporting (or absent) clause with a line reference, and — for anything not Present — paste a concrete replacement clause. Lead any review with this pillar; it carries the greatest penalty exposure.
