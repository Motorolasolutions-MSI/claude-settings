# Strategic Performance Requirements

Day-to-day filing is necessary but not sufficient. A broker that meets every transactional control can still let regulatory awareness, dispute handling, and license currency atrophy. These three controls keep the relationship strategically healthy.

## Checklist

### 1. Regular Audits & Reviews

- **Quarterly or semi-annual performance reviews** are written into the SOW with an agenda template covering, at minimum:
  - Compliance KPIs (timely-filing rate, exam rate, hold rate, PSC volume, §1592 events, prior disclosures)
  - Operational KPIs (response-time SLA adherence, after-hours response log)
  - Duty-spend analysis and FTA / duty-savings opportunities
  - Open audit items and corrective-action closure
  - Regulatory horizon items (new tariff actions, §301/232 changes, ADD/CVD activity touching the IOR's HTS codes)
- Reviews are **documented** — agenda, attendees, decisions, action items, owners, due dates. Verbal-only QBRs do not count.
- A **broker scorecard** is maintained between reviews so each meeting starts from data, not anecdotes.

### 2. Escalation Protocols

- The SOW defines a **clear, written escalation procedure** for:
  - Operational disputes (missed SLA, demurrage chargeback)
  - Compliance disputes (disagreement on classification, valuation, FTA eligibility)
  - Complex customs business issues (binding rulings, protests, §1520 reconciliation, prior disclosures, focused assessments)
- The escalation path names tiers, response-time targets at each tier, and the trigger conditions for moving between tiers.
- Endpoint is named — a specific compliance director or licensed broker of record on the brokerage side, and a named IOR counterpart.

### 3. Continuing Education

- Every individual licensed broker assigned to the account meets CBP's **mandated continuing education — 36 hours per triennial reporting period** (per 19 CFR §111.103, in effect since the 2024 triennial cycle).
- Broker provides, on request and at each review, evidence of:
  - Current license status (license number, current "active" status)
  - Triennial Status Report filing (the 19 CFR §111.30(d) report)
  - CE credit accrual to date (course names, providers, hours, dates)
- For the brokerage entity: at least one **qualifying licensed broker** is associated with each permitted district handling the IOR's entries (19 CFR §111.19).

## Suggested SOW language

> **Strategic Governance.** Broker and Importer shall conduct documented performance reviews not less than semi-annually, covering compliance and operational key performance indicators, duty spend, open audit items, and the regulatory horizon. Broker shall maintain a written escalation matrix identifying responsible individuals and response-time targets for operational, compliance, and complex-customs-business disputes. Broker shall ensure that each licensed customs broker assigned to Importer's account satisfies the continuing education requirement of 19 CFR §111.103 (36 hours per triennial period) and shall, on request, provide evidence of license status, Triennial Status Report filing, and CE credits.

## How to report findings

Score each of the three items **Present / Partial / Missing**. A missing escalation protocol is treated as **Medium-High** — it tends not to matter until a high-cost dispute arises, at which point its absence is unrecoverable. Missing CE evidence is **High** — an out-of-compliance individual broker can result in CBP suspending or revoking the license that the IOR's entries depend on.
